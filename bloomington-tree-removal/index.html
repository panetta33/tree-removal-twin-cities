Professional Tree Removal Across Bloomington

Bloomington is one of the largest and most diverse suburbs in the Twin Cities — about 90,000 residents across 38 square miles, from the older neighborhoods just south of Highway 494, to the post-war ranches along Old Shakopee Road, to the bluffs overlooking the Minnesota River. The city's tree population is just as varied: established silver maples and ash from the original 1950s and 60s plantings, mature oaks in the bluff neighborhoods, and a growing population of replacements wherever EAB and storms have taken older trees down.

What Makes Bloomington Tree Work Different

An aging canopy. A lot of Bloomington's residential street trees were planted in the postwar building boom and are now reaching the natural end of their service lives. Silver maples, in particular, become structurally compromised as they age — they grow fast, they grow big, and they develop weak branch attachments and internal decay. Many of the "still standing but worrying" trees in Bloomington are exactly the ones we recommend removing before they fail.

EAB and the ash population. Bloomington was an early Twin Cities EAB hotspot. The city has been removing public ash trees aggressively, and most private ash trees in Bloomington are infested or in late-stage decline. Standing dead ash is uniquely dangerous — the wood becomes extremely brittle and unpredictable. If you have a dead or dying ash, get it removed before it removes itself.

Bluff and slope lots. The Minnesota River bluff properties in southern Bloomington come with stunning views — and with steep slopes that complicate tree work significantly. Our crews are equipped for slope rigging and bluff-edge removals; not every "tree service" is.

Bloomington tree ordinances: the city requires permits for boulevard tree removal (trees in the right-of-way between sidewalk and street) and regulates work on certain heritage trees. We handle all permit research and applications as part of the quote.
