Professional Tree Removal Across Plymouth

With about 80,000 residents, Plymouth is one of the largest cities in Minnesota — and one of the most heavily-treed suburbs in the Twin Cities. From the wooded neighborhoods around Medicine Lake and Parkers Lake, to the newer subdivisions in northwest Plymouth, to the older streets near Plymouth Creek, this is a community whose property values depend on its trees as much as on anything else.

Tree Removal Twin Cities serves all of Plymouth — ZIPs 55441, 55442, 55446, and 55447 — with vetted local crews and the equipment to handle Plymouth's mix of mature canopy, lakeside lots, and storm-damaged trees.

The 2023 Derecho and Ongoing Storm Cleanup

On the night of August 28, 2023, Plymouth took some of the worst damage in the Twin Cities from a high-wind storm event that ripped through Hennepin County. Thousands of trees came down or were severely compromised; hundreds more were structurally damaged in ways that only became dangerous months or years later — split crotches, internal cracks, weakened root plates.

If your Plymouth property had storm damage in 2023, 2024, or 2025, there's a real chance one of those compromised trees is still standing and getting more dangerous with every freeze-thaw cycle. We're happy to do a free assessment to identify hidden hazards before another storm finishes the job.

Storm-damaged trees often pass insurance inspection but fail two years later. A tree that's structurally compromised but still standing is exactly the kind of thing your homeowner's policy may not cover when it eventually falls — because the damage was "pre-existing." A free assessment now can save you a major claim later.

Other Plymouth-Specific Tree Concerns

Emerald Ash Borer. Plymouth has lost a significant portion of its ash population to EAB, and the city has actively removed infested public trees. Most remaining private ash trees in Plymouth are infested or in decline. Dead ash is brittle and unsafe to leave standing — schedule removal before it becomes an emergency.

Oak wilt. Multiple oak wilt centers have been confirmed in Plymouth. Red oaks can die in one season; infected wood must be handled per DNR protocols. We follow those protocols on every oak removal.

Lakeside properties. Plymouth has several lake corridors (Medicine, Parkers, Bass, Mooney) where shoreline tree work may require coordination with watershed districts. We'll handle that for you.
