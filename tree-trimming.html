Tree Trimming & Pruning in the Twin Cities

Healthy, structurally-sound pruning that improves tree longevity, safety, and curb appeal.

Pruning Done Right Adds Decades to a Tree's Life

Good pruning isn't just cosmetic — it's preventative care. The right cuts in the right places improve a tree's structure, reduce storm-damage risk, clear hazards from your home and power lines, and let more light reach your lawn. The wrong cuts (or worse — "topping") can permanently weaken a tree and shorten its life.

Our local arborists follow ANSI A300 pruning standards. That means they know exactly what to remove, what to leave, and how to make cuts that the tree can heal cleanly.

When to Trim Your Trees

  • Dead, dying, or broken branches that could fall
  • Branches rubbing on your roof, siding, or fence
  • Limbs hanging over the driveway, sidewalk, or street
  • Branches contacting or threatening power lines
  • Crossing or rubbing branches inside the canopy
  • To thin a dense canopy for light and airflow
  • To shape young trees for strong long-term structure

Our Tree Trimming Services

We offer the full range of arborist pruning techniques:

  • Crown cleaning — removing dead, diseased, and weakly-attached branches
  • Crown thinning — selective removal to improve airflow and light penetration
  • Crown raising — clearing low branches for clearance over driveways, walkways, and lawn equipment
  • Crown reduction — reducing height or spread when a tree has outgrown its space
  • Deadwooding — removing dead limbs throughout the canopy
  • Structural pruning — shaping young trees for safety and longevity
  • Storm prep — selective pruning to reduce risk before Minnesota storm season
What we won't do: tree topping. Topping — cutting all branches back to stubs — is one of the worst things you can do to a tree. It causes rot, weak regrowth, and long-term decline. If a tree is too big for its space, we either reduce it correctly or recommend removal and replacement.

The Best Time to Prune in Minnesota

Most trees in the Twin Cities are best pruned in late winter or very early spring, before bud break. There are important exceptions:

  • Oaks — never prune April through July (oak wilt risk)
  • Elms — never prune April through October (Dutch Elm Disease risk)
  • Maples & birches — best pruned after leaf-out to avoid heavy sap bleeding
  • Storm damage or hazards — anytime, regardless of season
